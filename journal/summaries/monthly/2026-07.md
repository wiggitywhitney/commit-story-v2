# Monthly Summary — 2026-07

## Month in Review

The developer spent the month fixing and instrumenting the commit-story journaling system. Week 1 was about firefighting—environment variables were leaking into post-commit hooks and silently breaking LLM calls, stale placeholder files were blocking retries, and the dialogue-extraction prompt was too aggressive. They fixed the leaks across nine repositories, built a shared placeholder-detection helper, tightened the prompt, and eliminated a data-loss window in the journal-manager code. They also discovered the telemetry had stopped flowing (the OpenTelemetry Collector had crashed), and they promoted three critical telemetry attributes from recommended to required status to ensure the downstream dashboard would have what it needed.

With the immediate fires out, weeks 2 and 3 were about building observability into the system properly. The developer worked across 32 files to instrument the codebase with OpenTelemetry distributed tracing, covering commit collection, summary generation, journal creation, and AI interactions. They wrapped async functions with span calls, added relevant attributes, and built out the semantic conventions schema with 13 new attributes and 41 new span IDs. They were deliberate about what to instrument—skipping pure synchronous utilities and unexported helpers to avoid noise, focusing on async operations and I/O boundaries. They also caught and flagged several data quality issues: raw filesystem paths exposing internal structure, commit author fields leaking PII, and some async operations still missing spans. These got documented as follow-up work rather than blockers.

The month showed a clear progression from reactive fixes to proactive instrumentation. Week 1's cascade of interconnected problems made it clear the system needed better observability as a contract, not a nice-to-have. Weeks 2 and 3 built that foundation—14-15 core modules got traced, the schema got extended and validated, and the developer built mental models of what should and shouldn't be instrumented. The work wasn't friction-free (schema validation kept flagging attribute name mismatches and semantic duplicates), but the developer iterated through those issues and left the system with detailed tracing coverage across its major execution paths.

## Accomplishments

- Fixed environment variable leakage causing silent LLM failures in post-commit hooks across nine repositories, plus resolved cascading data integrity issues (stale placeholders, aggressive dialogue filtering, data-loss windows).
- Instrumented 14-15 core modules with OpenTelemetry distributed tracing, establishing observability into commit collection, summary generation, journal creation, and AI interactions.
- Extended the semantic conventions schema with 13 new attributes and 41 new span IDs, resolving validation conflicts through consolidation and careful namespace design.
- Promoted three critical telemetry attributes from recommended to required status, establishing a durable contract for downstream dashboard reliability.

## Growth

- Built mental models of OpenTelemetry schema design and validation constraints, learning when to consolidate duplicate definitions versus create new namespaces.
- Developed a clear philosophy about instrumentation scope—what to trace (async operations and I/O boundaries) versus what to skip (synchronous utilities, unexported helpers) to balance observability with noise reduction.
- Learned to identify and flag data quality concerns (PII, raw filesystem paths) for follow-up work rather than letting them block progress.

## Looking Ahead

- PII violations and data quality issues flagged but deferred: commit author fields need removal, raw filesystem paths should be shortened to basenames for privacy.
- Some async operations still missing spans—noted as candidates for companion instrumentation work.
- Filesystem paths and author information in trace attributes need refinement pending utility import availability.
